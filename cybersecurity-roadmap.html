Weekly rhythm (about 12 h)

  • 2 weekday evenings (2 h each): theory and courses
  • 2 weekday evenings (2 h each): labs (TryHackMe, HTB, PortSwigger)
  • Weekend (4 h): project work + one public writeup
  • Sunday (10 min): log your hours and tick off finished tasks

How progress is measured

  • Overall % is weighted by points, so a cert counts 4× a reading task.
  • Expected % comes from your start date (month 12 = 50%).
  • On track means you're within 5% of expected. Behind means 5–15% under, and off track means more than 15% under.
  • Skills snapshots show how your rating in each area grows month to month.

How your background helps

  • Python: automation, writing tools and detection logic, and reviewing code. Very few juniors bring this.
  • Linux: you can move through Phase 1 Linux topics quickly.
  • Docker: lets you build labs fast, and it leads straight into container and cloud security.
  • ERPNext/Frappe: a real business app you can pentest, harden and threat-model on your own machine. That makes portfolio projects nobody else has.

Rules

  • Only attack systems you own or are explicitly authorized to test (your lab, THM, HTB, PortSwigger).
  • Write about everything you finish. A GitHub repo or blog post is proof of skill.
  • Pick your specialization by month 18 at the latest. Don't spread yourself thin.
